The project centers around the duality of light and darkness in the city of Adana. The building exhibits both an introverted and extroverted character — internally focused through the museum, and externally connected through the public square. The contrast between light and shadow is expressed through a balanced relationship of solid and void, as well as hardscape and green space.

The luminous side of the building houses the café and administrative spaces, articulated through transparency and openness. In contrast, the darker side accommodates exhibition and cinema halls, enclosed and inward-facing in nature. The concept of the chimneys emerges from this dichotomy — they symbolize light within darkness. Inspired by Adana’s renowned tradition of pigeon keeping, the chimneys are designed to resemble pigeon lofts.

These sculptural chimneys rise dramatically over the square, aiming to evoke a sense of curiosity and excitement in users. They define a special interior space while allowing natural light to penetrate deep into the building. Additionally, they serve as passive ventilation elements.

Authenticity in materiality was a key concern throughout the design process. Real materials were employed to enhance the tangible and experiential quality of the architecture.
